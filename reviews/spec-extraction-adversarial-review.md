# Adversarial review of specification extraction for dependent inductives

20 November 2026. Reviewed the design at notes commit `3b42655` against CoqHammer `27b80e8`, built in the worktree's Rocq 9.2 switch with OCaml 5.3.0. Section numbers below refer to the design. [Design under review](../spec_extraction.md) · [Translator at the reviewed revision](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_transl.ml)

**Recommendation. Hold Stages 1 and 2 as defaults.** The useful operation is to specialize a constructor specification where it supplies evidence for a particular goal. Start with checked index matching and cheap consequences for asserted local inhabitants of regular `Set`/`Type` families. Preserve the current representation of constructor terms. A single-constructor record with two fields can benefit from exposing a pair of witnesses. Across sixteen occurrences of an eight-constructor family, the same policy produced 384 clauses and 128 witness symbols in a controlled probe. Sharing inversion took 40 clauses and eight witness symbols. Constructor count alone is a poor admission test.

Three issues need resolution before a general rollout. The proposed recursion test misses a cycle reproducible in today's translator. The proposed decomposition of index equations loses information in the target logic. Finally, changing every guard also changes the premises and conclusions of the declaration axioms on which the design relies. Fixing the first two and measuring selective expansion can proceed independently.

## 1. The expansion already has a recursive path through proofs

**Blocking, §§6, 9.2, 11.** Rocq accepts the following inhabited, strictly positive type. The constructor `rollE` has zero informative fields. Its proof payload asks for a witness of the original type.

```coq
From Hammer Require Import Hammer.
Inductive loopE : Set :=
| baseE : loopE
| rollE : (exists _ : loopE, True) -> loopE.
Definition e : loopE := baseE.
Hammer_transl "e".
```

`CEnum` calls `formulas` on the payload. Translating the existential calls `make_guard` on its bound variable, which classifies `loopE` again. The resulting formula contained the following number of nested existential binders. Only the OCaml stack limit changed. Counts and sizes refer to the single printed `$_typeof_...e` line, excluding its newline.

| Command prefix before `rocq c -time Inhabited.v` | Existentials | Characters | Time for `Hammer_transl` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `OCAMLRUNPARAM=l=32768` | 833 | 142,025 | 1.47 s |
| `OCAMLRUNPARAM=l=65536` | 1,673 | 286,399 | 5.00 s |

Both commands returned. The stack-dependent output falsifies the claim of structural termination through the existing side conditions. A regular type also exposes the Stage 1 problem. Rocq accepted `node : nat -> nat -> (exists _ : nest, True) -> nest`. Its current inversion actually contains an existential guarded by membership of `nest`. Both informative fields pass the proposed non-reachability test. Expanding that inner membership restarts the rule.

Spending a budget only along informative field types misses both paths. Carry the expansion context through payload translation, quantifiers, nested guards, normalization and side-axiom generation. Bound work per root as well as depth. In an acyclic chain where each record holds two copies of the preceding record plus a proof, the reachability test passes at every edge and the copied formula can double at each level. Memoizing a telescope saves translation time but does not bound the printed formula. Preserve existing cheap simplifications where justified, and use a real cutoff for re-entry. At a cutoff on a field of type `J (I n)`, the residual membership is for **that field type**, including `J`. The declaration inverse for `I` cannot consume it directly. [Guard and quantifier translation](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_transl.ml#L2140) · [Reachability implementation](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_erasure.ml#L335)

## 2. Solving an index must preserve the constructor term and the guard

**Blocking, §§4, 6, 9.1, 9.5.** Consider the regular family used in the prover probe below.

```coq
Parameter P : nat -> Prop.
Inductive packet : nat -> Set :=
| pack : forall n, nat -> nat -> P n -> packet n.
```

For `h : packet i`, eliminating the equation `i = n` leaves the constructor term **`pack(i,x,y)`**. The constructor still stores its index in its erased spine. Writing `pack(x,y)`, as a literal reading of `c(params, inf(c))` suggests, changes its symbol/arity and disconnects the equality from the match equations. The current dump uses a ternary constructor. Construct the full telescope in declaration order, substitute into the spine, and let term translation perform its usual proof erasure. Retain the instantiated field guard of a solved binder unless the surrounding assumptions justify dropping it. For example, eliminating `n` in `∃n. Nat(n) ∧ S(k)=S(n) ∧ B(n)` leaves `Nat(k) ∧ B(k)` under injectivity. A bare FOL subterm carries no sort certificate.

Likewise `∃a. w=c(a) ∧ P(a)` cannot be rewritten to `P(w)` by orienting the equation. Even an injective unary constructor can map a witness satisfying `P` to an object outside `P`. The existing `CSubset` equivalence depends on changing the *term representation* so the saturated constructor is its carrier. That choice also affects partial applications, lifted lambdas, matches, and declaration axioms. The current classifier checks, among other things, proof dependencies, a carrier at the head of a payload, recursion, and parameter-dependent indexed collapse. Keep the representation decision independent of which index patterns happen to solve at an occurrence. Otherwise `I (S k)` might acquire a carrier representation while `I i` and the function returning it still use a constructor. [Subset validation](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_erasure.ml#L421) · [Constructor conversion and partial applications](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_transl.ml#L2253) · [Existing indexed regression cases](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/tests/plugin/extraction_indexed.v)

**The same-head decomposition in §9.5 proves only one direction in general.** For retained parameters `A,B`, injectivity can give `d(A,x)=d(B,y) ⇒ x=y`. Congruence reconstructs the original equality only if the surviving parameter arguments agree too. The empty-payload case makes the error visible. Decomposing `nil(A)=nil(B)` into zero comparisons yields truth. A free constructor algebra can distinguish those terms. In a tiny FOF check with `∀A. nil(A)≠other`, Z3 returned `CounterSatisfiable` for the conjecture `nil(a)=nil(b)`. This refutes the stated **target-axiom** justification. Homogeneous source typing can supply the missing agreement in specific cases. Identify where that evidence survives erasure and where the surrounding telescope supplies it.

Restrict decomposition to saturated, aligned **translated** heads with certified injective payload positions and identical or independently justified remaining arguments. Otherwise solve any consequences justified by injectivity and retain the original equality under substitution. Clash needs certified disjoint representations, normally distinct constructors of the same data family. Source head inequality alone is insufficient across families, erased proofs, collapsed constructors, or unresolved applications. The current case-side `rigid_clash` inspects informative parameters too and normalizes both sides. It decides whether to omit an equation for a branch. Replacing a guard in a required axiom, in either polarity, demands a stronger justification. A shared traversal can support these different clients without giving them identical rewrite permissions.

Specify the result as failure-by-clash **or a pair** of substitution and residual equations. A constructor returning indices `(S n, f n, n)` at `(S k, j, k)` can both solve `n := k` and leave `j=f(k)`. Substitute capture-avoidably into earlier residuals, field guards, payloads, tags and dependent field types. Unknown heads, incomplete telescopes, opaque functions, partial applications and exhausted reduction budgets should preserve equations. Apply the viability test even when successful matching removes the last residual equation. At `Fin.t 0` all branches clash, and at `vec A (S k)` only the successor branch survives. A post-match filter requiring a remaining proof or equation could discard precisely that information. A matcher against rigid occurrence terms needs no general higher-order unification. Cache declaration analysis by parameters/context as today. Caching an index-specific solution under today's parameter-only occurrence key would reuse it at a different index. [Current matcher and case guardrails](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_transl.ml#L905) · [Fording and substitution into earlier equations](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_erasure.ml#L206)

## 3. Declaration axioms and occurrence guards have different jobs

**High, §§5, 8, 9.4, 10.1.** Write `M(w,i)` for the existing membership and `E(w,i)` for the expanded disjunction. Inversion supplies `M ⇒ E` under its parameter/index premises. Recovering `E ⇒ M` requires, branch by branch, constructor introduction at the same erased spine, field and parameter guards, equality transport by congruence, and a membership conclusion. A model proof for well-typed instantiations can license an optimization even if a selected ATP problem lacks some axioms. This leaves two separate obligations, semantic validity in the intended model and availability of a short proof in the emitted problem. In particular, replacing a negative guard by a weaker necessary consequence strengthens the enclosing premise and can produce false proofs.

The domain-closure obligation in §10.1 needs a quantifier, not just a syntactic label. In a domain with an inhabitant of the family and an unrelated junk object, `∀w. M(w,i) ⇒ E(w,i)` can hold while `∀w. E(w,i)` fails. An asserted `E(h,i)` is justified by the particular Rocq declaration of `h`; a function result needs the premises for its arguments. A fiber proved empty can yield false under the assumption of its inhabitant. Global false in a consistent context is a translation failure. Test this distinction by pairing a valid impossible-fiber elimination with a satisfiable problem containing an unrelated inhabitant.

The current inversion path itself translates the matched term's guard. Widening `guard_leaf` indiscriminately can turn the antecedent of the declaration inverse into `E`, and constructor typing can conclude `E(c(...),i)`. An opaque `M` left at a recursive cutoff, a type variable later specialized to this family, or an opaque alias then has no assured path to the specification. The converse problem occurs when a consumer still needs the membership of an explicitly constructed value. Retaining the *name* of the old inverse does not retain `M ⇒ E`. Use an explicit atomic-membership entry point in declarations that serve residual atoms, or keep membership and add a local consequence. Keep one-way lifted-product membership as well. An empty function domain makes its behavioral unfolding vacuously true. [Inversion construction](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_transl.ml#L3380) · [Function-membership regression](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/tests/plugin/membership_transl.v)

Occurrence expansion also gives no substitute for constructor **injectivity** in an equality goal. Take a two-element domain, set the field guard true, interpret `c` as constant, and define membership by `M(w) ↔ ∃x. w=c(x)`. Introduction, complete inversion and every expanded guard hold. `c(a)=c(b) ⇒ a=b` fails. Keep injectivity for represented constructors, including when every visible membership expands. Suppression for a globally erased carrier has a separate proof. Preserve per-constructor availability for introduction/injectivity, pairwise availability for discrimination, and completeness of the constructor list for exhaustiveness. Never interpret a filtered list as the whole declaration. [Axiom emission and selection checks](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/coq_transl.ml#L3212)

**Three corrections matter when choosing the baseline.**

- On this checkout, the note's exact telescope `tag : forall n, bool -> n < 5 -> depTag n` is already a `CSubset`. `Hammer_transl "depTag"` produced no inversion axiom. For a closed `h : depTag 3`, its typing says `(h=true ∨ h=false) ∧ 3<5`, up to trivial truth conjuncts. Use the two-data-field `packet` as the general-case baseline. Injection is tested per constructor, and the declaration skip for a subset bypasses its structural axioms. Constructor typing still has its own path.
- A field's **type** may be a proposition containing quantifiers, implications, matches or function variables. A positive closed enum assertion need not clausify to ground facts. A field containing `∀x. ∃y. R x y` introduces a Skolem function of `x` even when the outer inhabitant is a constant. A disjunction of ground conjunctions likewise supplies alternatives, not a unit fact per constructor.
- The usual `ev : nat -> Prop` is handled as a formula. A hypothesis of `ev 3` does not pass through a data guard on a proof object. Its relevant machinery is propositional inversion. Use `Fin.t 0`, `vec A (S k)` or a `Set`-valued evenness witness to test §9.5 on the guard path. `vec A i` with variable `i`, a genuinely dependent `sigT A B`, and an index headed by an opaque function all need residual structure. An arbitrary fiber or a recursive invariant generally supplies no witness-free local specification. One constructor layer supplies no induction principle.

The mask discrepancy of §10.3 is observable. With `Inductive indexed (A : Set) : A -> Set := mk : forall x : A, indexed A x.`, the declaration inverse compared the formal index to a quantified field and used `mk(A,x)`. At `hN : indexed nat 0` the tag was `mk(nat,0)`. At `hP : indexed True I` it was `mk(True)`. At `forall (P:Prop)(p:P), indexed P p` the output carried the premise/payload `P` and the same one-argument tag. Calling the same mask function on a formal `A` and on an instantiated proposition can legitimately give different answers. Specify the compatibility of the generated spines and which axioms apply at each arity. Sharing mask code alone cannot prove it. Include mixed data/proof instantiations in the *same* dump, especially with later fields whose types depend on the earlier field.

## 4. What the four solvers actually receive

The output is untyped TPTP FOF with equality. For the packet example, the concrete files have a binary family predicate `packet_$t(i,h)`, a ternary `pack_$a3(i,x,y)`, and arity/type bridges. The common membership path already has an indexable head specific to the family. Removing a guard can save instantiation, yet also remove a good resolution literal or SMT matching pattern. The output writer introduces arity and type definitions based on the symbols present in the whole problem. Count the written signature and the clausified one. A change that introduces zero names in the OCaml guard builder can still introduce names at both later stages. [TPTP writer](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/tptp_out.ml)

| Occurrence after taking account of conjecture negation | What search sees |
|---|---|
| Asserted `M(h,i)`, with ground parameters and quantifier-free branch bodies | One surviving constructor gives ground witness constants and often units. Several constructors give disjunctive clauses or fresh naming predicates. |
| Function result `∀u. D(u) ⇒ M(f(u),i(u))` | Witnesses can depend on `u`. Expanding several function typings duplicates witness functions. Generic inversion at the ground term `f(a)` may already suffice. |
| Lemma `∀w. M(w,i) ⇒ Q(w)` | A negative existential can become a universal. With a constructor equality, substitution can yield `∀x. D(x) ∧ P(x) ⇒ Q(c(x))` per branch, without a witness function. Using a known constructor can be cheap. Using an opaque local inhabitant may now require connecting it to a constructor. |
| Conjecture `∀w. M(w,i) ⇒ Q(w)` | Refutation asserts a counterexample, turning the guard positive. An existential conjecture flips the other way. Choose policy by role and actual polarity, including nested implications. |

For E and Vampire, short clauses, unit rewriting and an indexed family literal can be more valuable than saving one resolution step. Equalities involving a Skolem field and a constructor may cause superposition into unrelated occurrences. For Z3/CVC4 this is quantified uninterpreted-function reasoning with equality. A closed local expansion with atomic payloads can be handled with Boolean search and ground congruence closure. The *whole* problem still has quantified axioms, nonconstant constructors and frequently `happ`. It is generally outside QF_UF and function-free EPR. Neither the constructors nor `nat`, `S` and `<` automatically become SMT datatypes or linear arithmetic. Repeated inversion on a recursive tail can generate the next matching membership term even if the translator stops unrolling. E-matching, model-based instantiation and preprocessing heuristics make the practical effect prover-dependent. [SMT-LIB logic definitions](https://smt-lib.org/logics-all.shtml) · [Z3 quantifier mechanisms and matching loops](https://microsoft.github.io/z3guide/docs/logic/Quantifiers/)

Global SMT datatypes per index require a sort per fiber or an erasure plus guards to recover the index constraints. Datatype exhaustiveness on the shared junk sort would apply to unrelated values. Higher-order payloads such as quantification over predicates introduce applicative terms, guards and possibly lifts even in a shallow constructor expansion. Fresh selectors supply stable terms across occurrences and can align with a goal's projections, which is more than sharing their spelling. They also introduce total functions on junk and wrong constructors, and typically need quantified equations. Prefer existing source projections when they genuinely compute on the known constructor. Keep witnesses correlated via one tuple when no such projection is available. Independently obtaining `∃x. P(x)` and `∃y. Q(y)` cannot justify a relation between those values and the fields of this particular `h`.

## 5. Prover probes

**A real index-only specification.** With the `packet` declaration above, compile the following with the installed plugin. The baseline file here contained 2,745 FOF statements and 1,141,930 bytes.

```coq
Goal forall n, packet n -> P n.
intros n h.
hammer_dump "packet.p".
Abort.
```

I varied only the body of `$_typeof_h` in copies of that file. The **baseline** was `M(h,n)`. **Full local** was `∃x y. Nat(n) ∧ Nat(x) ∧ Nat(y) ∧ P(n) ∧ h=pack(n,x,y)`. **Index fact** was `M(h,n) ∧ P(n)`. These added 178 and 28 bytes respectively and kept the same inversion axiom, conjecture, names and background. This isolates the occurrence change. It is a hand specialization of a real dump, with no implementation of the proposal or corpus inference.

Wall times are medians of three interleaved runs, including file parsing. Commands used the plugin's prover arguments with a 4 s prover limit and a 6 s outer cutoff. Vampire ran in CASC mode with TPTP proof and axiom names, Z3 with `-c`, CVC4 with `--dump-unsat-cores-full`, E with `--auto-schedule -R --print-statistics -p --tstp-format`. The two witnesses bought nothing for this goal. The E schedule exhausted its one selected strategy on all three runs per variant. Direct E runs without auto-scheduling proved all three variants. Treat small timing differences as descriptive.

**A goal that needs both fields.** I also dumped `pairC : forall x y : nat, R x y -> pairOK` with `R : nat -> nat -> Prop`. Define `leftv` and `rightv` by matching on `pairC` and returning its first and second fields. The goal `forall h : pairOK, R (leftv h) (rightv h)` was dumped after introducing `h`, then checked in Rocq by `destruct h. assumption.` Its baseline had 2,979 FOF statements. The full-local edit used the two guarded witnesses and `h=pairC(x,y)`. A third edit added the source-derivable projection fact `R(leftv(h),rightv(h))` alongside membership. This is deliberately the best case for existing projections.

E, Vampire and Z3 proved all three variants under the same 4 s limit. CVC4 gave up on baseline and proved both local variants in each of three runs. Z3's baseline core used the inverse and both projection equations. Full local used both equations and omitted the inverse. The projection fact discharged the goal directly. This is a reason to keep a witness-carrying option for data-dependent payloads and to distinguish existing projections from newly axiomatized selectors. Neither experiment estimates end-to-end success rates.

**Independent clause-width probe.** For each `j=1..b`, let branch `B_j(w)` be `∃x. w=c_j(x) ∧ d_j(x) ∧ p_j(x)` with distinct heads, and let `E(w)` be their disjunction. Generate one shared axiom `∀w. t(w) ⇒ E(w)` and `n` facts `t(h_i)` for **shared**. For **inline**, generate just the `n` assertions `E(h_i)`. This gives inlining the advantage of omitting the shared inverse. Count the `cnf(` statements and distinct `sK`/ `sP` names printed by `vampire --mode clausify -updr off file.p`. ## 6. Implementation order and acceptance tests

1. **Establish the model obligations and fix re-entry first.** State where `M ↔ E` is valid, under which guards on parameters and indices, and which direction each optimization uses. Exercise the inhabited cycle above, mutual recursion, higher-order fields, proof-field dependencies and an acyclic branching record chain. Assert closure, an explicit output bound and stable translation across stack limits. Check that a missing telescope, opaque alias, WHNF cutoff, or partial application leaves an atomic fallback with a usable introduction/elimination path.
2. **Implement conservative matching on existing representations.** First measure bare-variable elimination in shared inversion and safe guard-side pruning on current enums/subsets. Test `0` versus `S k`, `S n` versus `S k`, repeated indices, solved plus stuck equations in either order, and parameter/proof fields with varying arity. Preserve matches that genuinely reach their branches. Use both positive and negative guards with well-typed witnesses. Include a true construction goal, an impossible fiber, a satisfiable junk case, a constructor-injectivity goal without membership hypotheses, and a polymorphic consumer of an opaque membership. A timeout on a false conjecture cannot establish soundness.
3. **Add the smallest useful positive consequence.** For an asserted regular inhabitant of known type, specialize against the indices. Retain witness-free conjuncts that follow in every viable branch, or a short disjunction of the branch-specific consequences. If a surviving branch contributes truth, the resulting disjunction may say nothing. Keep existing membership for consumers. Never insert a merely necessary consequence as the entire negative guard. If one or few viable branches carry a relation on actual data, try a bounded local existential or verified existing projections. For generic `vec A i`, wide symbolic sums, nested quantified payloads, or repeated occurrences, prefer the shared inverse until measurement shows a gain. Gate on surviving branch count, residual binders, estimated literals, polarity and repetition after matching. The mere presence of a proof field is a poor proxy for value.
4. **Trace the proof back to Rocq.** The two-literal SMT core above contains `$_typeof_h` and the conjecture. `get_atp_info` records `h` under typings, while `get_tac_args` derives local `used_ids` from dependencies and inversion hints from inversion/case names. With `clear_unused` on, a non-section `h : packet n` can be cleared because the goal `P n` mentions only `n`. I replayed the saved Z3 baseline and full-local cores through the evaluation `hammer_hook` in reconstruction mode, at the default tactic time limit. Both failed with `Set Hammer ClearUnused`. With `Unset Hammer ClearUnused`, baseline reconstructed with `hauto` and full local with `qblast`. This exposes an existing failure on a goal where retaining one local value suffices. For reproduction, put a saved Z3 result in `atp/o/z3-knn-32/test.p`, write `reconstr` to `coqhammer.opt`, and call `hammer_hook "probe" "test"` after `intros n h` in the packet context. Use a fresh test name for each run so the hook does not reuse its output. Retain owners of used local typings and the source family to invert when needed. This provenance can travel in the translation metadata without adding a logical predicate. Test a successful ATP proof **and** reconstruction with hypothesis clearing enabled. [ATP name classification](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/atp_names.ml) · [Reconstruction and hypothesis clearing](https://github.com/lukaszcz/coqhammer/blob/27b80e8/src/plugin/hammer_main.ml#L378)
5. **Screen competing changes separately.** Compare baseline, matching alone, shared-inversion simplification, local index facts, and bounded full local expansion on identical premises, budgets and prover versions. Stratify index-only, payload-dependent, impossible-index and ordinary nondependent goals. Record translation time, FOF bytes, clause/literal counts, witness arities and term depths, solver-specific wins/losses and timeouts, proof-core owners, and common goals reconstructed by any prover. Repeat near the cutoff and inspect regressions on existing goals. The previous indexed-family evaluation already gained 516 and lost 814 common ATP cells despite a slight *decrease* in mean input bytes. A new fixture or a shorter dump cannot offset an unexplained old-goal regression. [Existing seven-corpus evaluation](../extraction_eval.md)

The first candidate I would benchmark is index matching plus local index facts for regular families, with local-typing provenance preserved for reconstruction. The two-field record is the control that should decide whether witness-carrying expansion earns its additional cost.
